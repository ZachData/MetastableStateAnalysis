## 6. Is "the phases are interesting but not core" a good read?

Mostly yes. The test is whether a phase measures a **global** quantity on the training axis (keep) or a **local** object (a head, a feature, one cluster: set aside). It also matters whether a phase is set aside for **low value** or only because it is **blocked**:

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 energy violations, dissipation | yes | **core** | — |

## 6a. Methods: from a global energy to mechanisms

Added 2026-09-29 at the user's request: which methods isolate *what* carries
the anti-collapse force, and how the SLT view (RLCT, estimated as the LLC)
fits. Tier 1; nothing run.

**The tool already in the repo.** Pythia's parallel residual makes the
dissipation identity split each layer's energy change *exactly* into per-head
and per-MLP terms, with no cross term (`PROJECT.md` §3.8.3: per-head sum to
1e-5). Most attribution methods are not exact. Three steps turn §4.1 from
global into mechanistic:
1. **Localise:** per head, per MLP, per checkpoint, which components ascend
   E_β (push apart) and which descend it, and when each turns on. Tier B ran at
   β = 1 and was never read this way; rerun at the measured β.
2. **Test causally:** mean-ablate the top anti-collapse components (mean, not
   zero: `PROJECT.md` §3.15). Does energy become monotone again, do tokens
   re-cluster, what does it cost in loss? This is the first circuit-level
   claim available: "these N components are the anti-collapse mechanism".
3. **Known answer:** in `repeated_tokens` identical tokens start collapsed;
   which components separate them, and from which step.

**Methods from outside, ranked:**

| # | method | what it answers | cost | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Train small models ourselves** (2–4 layers, d ≈ 64–256, synthetic data with known structure) | turns "SGD + data → structure" into an experiment: we choose data (Markov chains, repeated patterns), optimiser (Adam / SGD / Muon: `FUTURE_IDEAS.md` D5 without public checkpoints), weight decay (which controls sinks, per Gu et al.), checkpoint density (`p8_scale_ladder/status-8.md` invariant 3 needed it) | CPU-feasible at this size; the fleet box is too small | **the obvious addition**; the only way to vary data and optimiser with everything else held fixed |
| M2 | **Data-statistics milestones** per checkpoint: KL of the model to unigram / bigram / trigram predictors on held-out text | does the energy break at 128–512 fall where the model leaves unigram for bigram statistics? The "data structure SGD implements" link, measured | forward passes on 410m, about Stage 0's cost | high. Belrose et al. 2024 (statistics of increasing complexity, on Pythia) is cited from memory, unopened |
| M3 | **LLC per checkpoint, and per head** (refined LLC; Wang et al. 2024, from memory; `devinterp`) | do LLC stage boundaries fall at 512 and 1000–2000; do high-rLLC heads match the heads step 1 flags? | SGLD, many forward and backward passes: plausible on 70m / 160m on CPU, heavy on 410m | high, after step 1 localises; this is the SLT bridge |
| M4 | **Lyapunov exponents of the depth map** (random-probe Jacobian-vector products per layer) | which directions a layer stretches or shrinks token differences: a frame-free anti-collapse measure with no clustering, splittable into attention and MLP | autograd on CPU, moderate | medium-high; `PUBLICATION_IDEAS.md` Idea 2's instrument |
| M5 | **Parameter decomposition** (APD / SPD) | a weight-space account of the mechanism | scaled to small transformers; on 410m unclear | **later**, on the components step 1 names or on M1's models; on all of Pythia it would cost the quarter |
| M6 | Susceptibilities (Timaeus) | per-token response of the posterior to data | GPU | parked (`FUTURE_IDEAS.md` D6) |

**On the SLT frame.** It fits the question's shape: the data's structure
selects which algorithms the weights implement, and the posterior concentrates
on degenerate (simpler) solutions. Caveat: SLT describes the posterior at a
point, not Adam's path (`FUTURE_IDEAS.md` §2 C), and this repo's strongest
signals are transitions along the path. So test three views on one axis and see
where they agree:

| view | predicts | tested by |
|---|---|---|
| SLT | stage boundaries are LLC changes | M3 |
| Distributional simplicity | transitions line up with n-gram milestones | M2 |
| Optimiser dynamics (Adam's implicit bias, outlier coordinates, sinks) | part of the structure is the optimiser's, not the data's | M1 (swap optimiser), `FUTURE_IDEAS.md` D8 |

If the energy break, the OV repulsive phase, an LLC stage and the bigram
milestone coincide, that is the paper. If they separate, which ones move
together is the result.

**Order:** (1) per-component dissipation at measured β, then mean-ablation;
(2) M2 and `FUTURE_IDEAS.md` D1/D3/D4 on the same checkpoint axis, one
timeline across data, weights and activations; (3) M1, repeating the pipeline
with data and optimiser varied; (4) M3, then M5, only on what (1)–(3) point at.
Only (3) needs new infrastructure.
