## 0. The five things it changes

1. **The count law is `Θ(β^((d−1)/2))`, and the paper itself conjectures the
   effective-dimension version.** `math-10.md` §5.2 derived a `d_eff` regression
   as a rescue; it is the paper's own open conjecture (§5, p. 6). That upgrades
   the test from a workaround to a direct empirical attack on a named open
   problem.
2. **Lemma C.1 gives an exact, distribution-free, dimension-general count for
   strong Rényi centers — and it does not grow with `n`.** It saturates. **That
   is this project's carrying-capacity finding** (max-alive clusters invariant at
   50–55) with a formula attached. *(2026-09-24: on Stage 0's 410m runs that
   finding is mostly a repeated-token count at the embedding layer, so the match
   is in doubt: `p10_cluster_function/status-10.md` §1.8.)*
3. **Theorem 4.1: all tokens converge to `x₁(0)` — the first token's initial
   position — for arbitrary `Q, K` and `V = Id`.** The first token is autonomous
   and is the attractor. The position-0 story is a theorem, not a confound.
4. **The paper says a trainable RMSNorm diagonal is absorbable into `K, Q, V`.**
   So Phase 9's γ lever is not a read-side lever — it hits `W_Q`, `W_K` **and**
   `W_V` together. `plan-9.md` §7 is wrong on this and §4.7's sign test cannot be

5. **Explicit timescales.** Quasi-stationarity for `exp(c²/2)`; final collapse at

   `lit-1.md` §4 item 4's "trapping timescale as a quantitative prediction",
   filed as *blocked on reading the paper*, is unblocked.

## 1. The model (§2)

Full attention, Geshkovski et al.'s (SA):

```
    ẋ_k = P_{x_k} [ (1/Z_k) Σ_{j=1}^{n} e^{β⟨Qx_k, Kx_j⟩} V x_j ],
    Z_k = Σ_{j=1}^{n} e^{β⟨Qx_k, Kx_j⟩}
```

Causal (CSA) replaces both sums with `Σ_{j=1}^{k}` — token `k` sees only `j ≤ k`.

**The remark that matters for Phase 9**, §2 verbatim:

> "(RMSNorm layer usually also includes a multiplication by a trainable diagonal
> matrix `D`, but **the effect of this step can be equivalently achieved by
> multiplying `K, Q, V` matrices by `D`**.)"

Also: `t` corresponds to **network depth**, `x_k(t)` to the representation of
token `k` at layer `t`.

## 2. The first token is autonomous, and is the limit (§3, §4)

§3: *"in (CSA) dynamics, **the first token is evolving fully autonomously**
without the influence of others."*

**Theorem 4.1.** Let `V = Id` and `Q, K` be **arbitrary**. Then for almost any
starting point on `(S^{d−1})^n`, the (CSA) dynamics converge to a single cluster:

```
    ∀k ∈ [n],   lim_{t→∞} x_k(t) = x₁(0)
```

Strictly stronger than the full-attention analogue: Geshkovski et al. need
`QᵀK = V`; Markdahl et al. need `QᵀK = Id`. **Here there are no constraints on
`K`, `Q` or `β` at all.** The proof needs the weight functions only to be
positive and `C¹`, which the paper notes suggests time-dependent `Q, K` would
not break it.

**§4's headline, in the paper's words:** *"The surprising result here is that
only the spectral properties of `V` (and not `K` or `Q`) affect asymptotics."*
Table 1 is the conjectured atlas, keyed on `λ_max(V)` and its eigenspace `L`:

| `λ_max` | multiplicity | final configuration |
|---|---|---|
| `> 0` | `d` | **the first particle `x₀`** |
| `> 0` | `≥ 2` | one point in `L` |
| `> 0` | `1` | two points `ξ`, `−ξ` |
| `< 0` | `≥ 2` | point cloud around `L` |
| `< 0` | `1` | two point clouds around `ξ`, `−ξ` |

Row 1 is Theorem 4.1; the rest are conjectures. Two forces drive each token:
an **internal** one (Lemma 3.1) and an **external** one from preceding
particles, *"either attractive or repulsive depending on the sign of the top
eigenvalue(s) of `V`"*, with attention setting the balance.

## 3. No gradient flow (§4)

Verbatim, and this is the abstract's claim with its consequence spelled out:

> "our system (CSA) **does not have a gradient-flow structure** and thus
> techniques of Łojasiewicz are not applicable. On the other hand, we have a
> significant advantage in the **hierarchical structure** of our system, allowing
> us to study tokens sequentially."

So `plan-9.md` §5.1a's hazard is confirmed from primary text. **What replaces the
lost structure is hierarchy — sequential, position-ordered analysis** — which is
the same axis §5's parking construction runs on.

## 4. Rényi parking, exactly (§5.1)

Sequence `(x_j)_{j≥1}` on `S^{d−1}` with geodesic distance `dist`, separation
parameter `δ > 0`:

- **Rényi centers**: the subsequence `(x_{s_j})` with
  `dist(x_{s_j}, x_{s_i}) > δ` for all `i < j` — separated from **previous
  centers**.
- **Strong Rényi centers**: `dist(x_{s_j}, x_i) > δ` for all `i < s_j` —
  separated from **all previous particles**. A subset of the above.

**The "car length" is `δ ~ β^{-1/2}`**, and the reason is §A.2: *"particles in
our system exert maximal attractive force at distances of order at most
`β^{-1/2}`, with rapid decay beyond this scale."* The paper's figures use
`δ = 4β^{-1/2}`.

**The arrival order is token order.** The street is the sphere; the cars are
tokens in sequence; a token parks (becomes a centre) iff it lands `δ`-far from
what is already there. Hence *"initialization irregularities generate
distinctive tokens, termed Rényi parking centers, which **evolve into
meta-stable cluster nuclei**"*, and *"strong Rényi centers serve as primary
attractors for subsequent tokens."*

**Both occur with frequency `Θ(β^((d−1)/2))`**, confirming the `√β` scaling
predicted for `d = 2`.

**The two kinds behave differently and both are useful:** Rényi centers *"better
capture the meta-stable clustering effect"* but *"lack positional stability and
may converge to other centers over time"*; strong centers are visually
stationary *"but do not explain all clusters"* (Fig. 2 caption).

**The generalisation Phase 9 should note:** *"While defined here using geodesic
distance on a sphere, the definition **extends naturally to distances induced by
`⟨Qx, Ky⟩`** under appropriate conditions. This generalization aligns with our
observation that meta-stable clustering occurs in the subspace `L` where `V`
sends tokens and acts as the identity on `L`."*

### 4.1 Lemma 5.1 — quasi-stationarity, and it decays with token index

`d = 2`, `Q = K = V = I₂`, strong centres separated by `c(1+2ε)β^{-1/2}`. Under
condition (2) — *"for `β > 1` a sufficient condition is simply `c > 1`"* — the
displacement of each centre stays below `εcβ^{-1/2}` for any `T_j` with
`T_j s_j h(cβ^{-1/2}) < εcβ^{-1/2}`, and a sufficient condition is

```
    T_j · s_j  <  e^{c²/2 − c⁴/(24β)} · ε
```

Two readings the paper draws: *"indexes `s_j` are mostly small. Thus, we see that
**early strong Rényi centers are almost stationary for a time that is exponential
with the square of separation magnitude**."* And note the `s_j` on the left:
**the stationarity time is inversely proportional to the centre's token index.**
Later centres are stationary for proportionally less time.

### 4.2 Theorem 5.2 — frozen centres capture everything

`d = 2`, `K = Q = V = I₂`. If `m` tokens `θ_j` are **artificially frozen**
(*"analogous to cross-attention in encoder-decoder architectures"*) and
well-separated (`|θ_i − θ_j| > cβ^{-1/2}`), then all other tokens converge to
their vicinity. The paper is explicit that this is *"a weaker result"* than
meta-stable clustering, because real centres are only quasi-stationary.

### 4.3 Lemma C.1 — the exact count, and it does not grow with `n`

> In an infinitely long sequence, **the average number of strong Rényi centres is
> the inverse spherical cap surface area `1/σ_{d−1}(B_δ)`.** It grows as
> `1/δ^{d−1}`, and
>
> ```
>     1/σ_{d−1}(B_δ) =  π/δ                  (d = 2)
>                       (3 sin²(δ/2))^{-1}   (d = 3)
> ```

Proved for **any spherically symmetric `μ`, in any dimension** — the paper notes
strong centres are *"much easier to handle"* than ordinary Rényi centres, whose
`d = 2` count is the classical Rényi result (`c·2π/δ` with the Rényi constant
`c ≈ 0.75`, via Dvoretzky–Robbins 1964) and which are *"significantly harder in
higher dimensions"*.

**Consistency:** with `δ = cβ^{-1/2}`, `1/δ^{d−1} = c^{-(d−1)} β^{(d−1)/2}` —
the §5 frequency.

> **The count is a limit over an infinite sequence: it SATURATES in `n`.** More
> tokens do not buy more centres once the sphere is full. That is the exact
> shape of this project's own unexplained finding — **max simultaneously-alive
> clusters invariant at 50–55 across all 27 checkpoints** — and it is where the
> 0.7476 constant actually lives (in the `d = 2` *ordinary*-centre count, not in
> anything this project measures).

## 5. The effective-dimension conjecture (§5, p. 6) — the paper's own open problem

Verbatim:

> "This section restricts our analysis to the case where `V = Id`. For general
> matrices `V`, our empirical observations suggest that **particles rapidly
> converge to a lower-dimensional subspace spanned by `d₁ ≪ d` principal
> eigenvectors. Consequently, we conjecture that the number of meta-stable
> clusters should rather be `β^((d₁−1)/2)`, where the ambient dimension `d` is
> replaced by the effective dimension `d₁`.** While a rigorous proof of this
> dimension-reduction remains an open problem for future investigation, this
> phenomenon motivates our focus on low-dimensional cases (specifically `d = 2`)."

**`math-10.md` §5.2's regression — slope `a = (d_eff − 1)/2`, invariant to β's
unit convention, returning `d_eff = 2a + 1` — is a direct empirical test of this
conjecture.** It was derived independently as a way around the `d = 1024`
problem; it is the paper's own proposed resolution of the same problem.

**And `d₁` is not a free parameter here: it is the dimension of `L`, the top
eigenspace of `V`.** Phase 2's `sym_*` / `schur_*` projectors are exactly what
identifies that subspace, and they are on disk for all 19 checkpoints. So the
test is **differential**: predict `d₁` from the OV spectrum, then check the
slope against it.

## 6. Limitations, in the paper's own words (§6)

- Lemma 5.1 gives quasi-stationarity for `exp(c²/2)`; choosing `c ~ β^ε` gives
  time exponential in `β`. **But** *"this falls short of proving meta-stable
  clustering, as Theorem 5.2 provides no bounds on the convergence time."*
- *"A complete meta-stability theory would require demonstrating that each Rényi
  center captures `Ω(n)` particles in `O(1)` time as `n → ∞`. Currently, even
  the weaker claim of capturing `ω(1)` particles remains unproven."*
- Practical: **tied weights across layers** (Pythia is not tied), and **the
  omission of the MLP layer** — *"Incorporating the MLP dynamics into our
  theoretical framework remains a significant open challenge."*
- Meta-stable states, not final ones, are what matters at realistic depth:
  final states are reached at `t = exp(Ω(√β))`, and *"the time parameter in our
  dynamics corresponds to network depth"*.

  the gap §6 names and directly Phase 10 §7's question.
