\title{When a language model's interaction energy stops rising with depth}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In the particle picture of self-attention, tokens are particles on a sphere and, under identity weights, their interaction energy $E_\beta $ never decreases with depth. We count the layer-to-layer drops in $E_{1}$ at 18 checkpoints of \texttt{pythia-410m} and of its nine PolyPythias seeds, on seven prompts. The count is zero through step 128 on seed 0 and reaches half its early plateau at step 512; a prediction registered before the seeds' intermediate checkpoints were downloaded put each seed's break at 256 or 512, and it held on 9 of 9. $E_{1}$'s first-order term is half the squared norm of the tokens' mean, and $E_{1}$ moves with it: at every checkpoint the two change in the same direction at 0.96 or more of the layer-to-layer transitions, so the count is close to a count of the layers at which the mean's norm falls. With each prompt's mean token removed, the count breaks later, at 1000 on seed 0 and on eight of the nine seeds, the step at which the previous-token and induction heads are dated. Editing every head's OV map (its symmetric part, its rotation removed, or its step-128 map) does not undo the break. Every dated event falls inside the learning-rate warmup, and neither a different peak rate (\texttt{pythia-70m}, \texttt{pythia-160m}) nor a different warmup (Mistral's GPT-2 small) separates the dates from the schedule. The results are exploratory except the registered seed count, on one model size, seven hand-picked prompts and a grid with no checkpoint between 512 and 1000.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

Read a transformer's depth as time and its tokens as particles on a sphere, and self-attention becomes an interaction between them \citep{geshkovski2023}. Under the simplest weights (one head with Q = K = V = I, the same in every layer) the particles attract and cluster, and the interaction energy $E_\beta $ of \S\ref{sec:2.1} never decreases with depth. That picture organises a growing theory of clustering and metastability (\S\ref{sec:8}). Trained language models do not follow it: at the end of its training, \texttt{pythia-410m}'s $E_{1}$ falls between consecutive layers 70 times over our seven prompts, out of 24 $\times $ 7 layer-to-layer transitions. No theorem is broken, since none covers a trained model (\S\ref{sec:2.1}), but the picture's prediction fails, and nothing says when along training it starts to fail, or what else is changing then.

We read $E_{1}$ layer by layer at 18 checkpoints of \texttt{pythia-410m} and of nine retrained seeds, counting V, the drops between consecutive layers (\S\ref{sec:3}). We report two dated events, their place on the model's developmental order, and one negative result:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The break, dated and reproduced (\S\ref{sec:4}).} Through step 128 $E_{1}$ is monotone in depth on every prompt (V = 0 on seed 0); V reaches half its 1000--4000 level at step 512. A prediction registered before any seed's intermediate checkpoints were downloaded put the break at 256 or 512 on each of nine PolyPythias seeds, and it falls there on 9 of 9. The date is the recipe's, not one run's; with seeds that share the recipe this was the expected outcome, and the registration guards only against choosing the window after seeing them (\S\ref{sec:3.4}).
  \item \textbf{The token-centred cloud breaks later (\S\ref{sec:4.1}).} $E_{1}$'s first-order term is half the squared norm of the tokens' mean (\S\ref{sec:2.1}). With each prompt's mean token removed before normalising, the centred count at 512 on seed 0 is 0.19--0.24 of its own late level, where the uncentred count is at 0.98 of its own; the centred cloud's break comes at 1000, on seed 0 and on eight of the nine seeds at every tolerance read: one grid step after the break on the seeds that break at 512, two on those that break at 256. That is the step at which the previous-token and induction heads are dated. The two counts' sizes are not compared, only their dates. What the mean carries is read from $E_{1}$'s series instead, after the dates: at every checkpoint $E_{1}$ and $\|m\|^{2}$ change in the same direction at 0.96 or more of the transitions, so V nearly counts the layers at which the mean's norm falls, at the break (each of its 1224 drops from step 128 to 1000 is one) and everywhere else along training.
  \item \textbf{The timeline (\S\ref{sec:5}).} On seed 0 the break comes after the token cloud's rank collapse (step 8) and the unigram minimum (128); the bigram minimum (256), the fall in the OV maps' attractive share (512) and the previous-token and induction heads (1000) are each within one grid step of it, which we count as aligned, not ordered, since these are different kinds of quantity dated by different statistics (item 2's two counts are one statistic, \S\ref{sec:4.1}). \textbf{``Break, then induction, then the OV maps' repulsive phase'' is not shown:} in either reading of that phase it ties the break or induction on this grid.
  \item \textbf{One intervention, no reversal (\S\ref{sec:6}).} Making every head's OV map symmetric, or removing its rotation, does not take the break back towards monotone at any step after it; giving every head its own step-128 OV map lowers V at 512 and 1000 but leaves it far from monotone. The break is neither shown to be the OV maps' nor shown not to be.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What none of this separates (\S\ref{sec:7}).} Every dated event from step 128 to 1000 falls inside the learning-rate warmup, which runs to step 1430 and which all ten runs share. Two further runs do not separate a training event from the schedule. On \texttt{pythia-70m} and \texttt{pythia-160m}, which warm up over the same steps at higher peak rates, both dates are consistent with the count of updates and with the cumulative learning rate, and the centred date excludes the rate itself. On Mistral's GPT-2 small, whose warmup is logarithmic and longer, the break's larger rise falls where the count of updates predicts, after a smaller first rise where the cumulative rate does, and the label the rule fixed before that run gives turns on two of its choices (the plateau window and one tolerance of the centred count). So the dates are dates inside a warmup, not training events told apart from it.

Everything here is exploratory, on one model size, seven hand-picked prompts and a grid with no checkpoint between 512 and 1000; only the seed count of \S\ref{sec:4} was registered. \S\ref{sec:2} gives the energy and the exact conditions under which it is monotone, \S\ref{sec:3} the setup, \S\ref{sec:8} related work; appendix~\ref{sec:A} reads the heads' shared subspaces along the same steps, appendix~\ref{sec:B} the gate each reading passed before it was read, and appendix~\ref{sec:C} what the count is made of (by prompt, by layer, without the frame-changing transitions, and $E_{1}$'s net change).

\section{Background}\label{sec:2}

\subsection{The energy, and when it is monotone in depth}\label{sec:2.1}

Put each token's residual-stream state on the unit sphere, $x_{i} \in S^{d-1}$, i = 1, \dots{}, n, and let

\begin{equation}
E_\beta(X) = \frac{1}{2\beta n^2}\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\exp\bigl(\beta\,\langle x_i, x_j\rangle\bigr).
\end{equation}

$E_\beta $ is large when the tokens are close together and is maximised by a single cluster; over probability measures on the sphere its minimiser is the uniform one \citep[Prop. 3.4]{geshkovski2023}. Read a transformer's depth as continuous time for an interacting-particle system, with self-attention the interaction. When every layer has the same single head with Q = K = V = I, the dynamics are a gradient ascent of $E_\beta $ in a metric that reweights the tokens, so $E_\beta $ is non-decreasing in depth \citep[\S{}3.2]{geshkovski2023}; with V = $-$I it is non-increasing. Wider hypotheses give monotonicity of a different, weighted energy, not of $E_\beta $: for one head whose score matrix $Q^{\top}$K is symmetric and equal to V, the dynamics ascend (1/2$\beta $) $\sum _{ij}$ exp($\beta \langle Vx_{i}, x_{j}\rangle $) in a metric that weights each token by its attention normaliser \citep[\S{}3.4.1]{geshkovski2023}, and for many heads whose score matrices are each symmetric and equal (or close) to their value maps, a sum of energies weighted by those matrices is non-decreasing \citep[Theorems 11--12]{pendharkar2026}, though a single head's own energy can fall through cross-head terms \citep[Theorem 17]{pendharkar2026}. No result covers MLP blocks, values untied from the scores, weights that change from layer to layer, or discrete layers, and trained transformers satisfy the symmetry and tying conditions nowhere exactly \citep[\S{}10]{pendharkar2026}.

So no theorem says a trained language model's $E_\beta $ should be monotone in depth, and none says a randomly initialised one's should. We read monotonicity as the prediction of the identity-weight picture, and a drop in $E_\beta $ between consecutive layers as a departure from that picture, not as the violation of a theorem. That a model at initialisation is (almost) monotone, and a trained one is not, is then an empirical fact about this model, which \S\ref{sec:4} dates.

$E_\beta $ sums over all pairs of tokens, so its first-order term is set by the tokens' mean. With m = (1/n) $\sum _{i} x_{i}$, expanding the exponential gives, exactly,

\begin{equation}
E_1 = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\,\|m\|^2 + \sum_{k\ge 2}\frac{1}{2n^2\,k!}\sum_i\sum_j \langle x_i, x_j\rangle^k,
\end{equation}

where $\|m\|^{2}$, the squared norm of the mean, is a standard measure of anisotropy (\S\ref{sec:8}). The higher terms are not small (|$\langle x_{i}, x_{j}\rangle $| $\leq $ 1, and the n diagonal terms are 1), so $E_{1}$ is not $\|m\|^{2}$, but a drop in $E_{1}$ between layers can come from the mean shrinking alone. \S\ref{sec:4.1} removes the mean.

\subsection{The developmental order}\label{sec:2.2}

Language models acquire their internal structure in stages. In a small two-layer attention-only model, \citet{hoogland2024} find bigram statistics first, then positional use and common 3- and 4-grams, then previous-token heads, then induction heads, then a long stage with no significant change, each stage a period of change in the local learning coefficient. Induction heads form at the point where in-context learning improves abruptly, visible as a bump in the training loss \citep{olsson2022}. On Pythia, a model's loss on sequences sampled from unigram and bigram statistics falls to a trough between steps $2^{6}$ and $2^{8}$ and rises until about $2^{10}$ \citep{belrose2024}, and task-level components emerge at similar token counts across model sizes \citep{tigges2024}. \S\ref{sec:5} places the energy's break on this order for one model, \texttt{pythia-410m}, with every row dated by the same locator.

\subsection{Pythia's training, and its warmup}\label{sec:2.3}

The Pythia models are trained for 143 000 steps of 1024 sequences of 2048 tokens \citep{biderman2023}, with the learning rate warmed up linearly over the first 1 \% of training (1430 steps) and decayed by a cosine to 0.1 of its peak; the peak is 3 $\times 10^{-4}$ for \texttt{pythia-410m}, 6 $\times 10^{-4}$ for \texttt{pythia-160m} and $10^{-3}$ for \texttt{pythia-70m} \citep{biderman2023}, and the released training configs give the two smaller models the same warmup (1430 steps for 160m, 1430 for 70m). The suite releases checkpoints at steps 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512 and then every 1000 steps. Every early event \S\ref{sec:4} and \S\ref{sec:5} date, the energy's break included, therefore falls inside the warmup, and nothing in one schedule separates a change caused by training from one caused by the schedule. \citet{li2025} tie the first of their three geometric phases to the same ramp. PolyPythias \citep{vanderwal2025} retrains \texttt{pythia-410m} from nine further seeds with the same schedule, which lets \S\ref{sec:4} ask whether the break's step is the run's or the recipe's, but not whether it is the schedule's (\S\ref{sec:7}).

\section{Setup}\label{sec:3}

\subsection{The model, the checkpoints and the prompts}\label{sec:3.1}

\texttt{pythia-410m} has 24 layers of width 1024, each with 16 attention heads (384 in all); each block adds its attention and its MLP to the residual stream in parallel, and positions enter through rotary embeddings \citep{biderman2023}. We read 18 of its checkpoints, steps 0, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1000, 2000, 4000, 8000, 16 000, 32 000, 54 000, 143 000. Step 1 is left out: in our runs its outputs are identical to step 0's (we did not compare the weights), so it adds no point. There is no checkpoint between 512 and 1000, or between 1000 and 2000, so nothing below dates a change more finely than the grid.

PolyPythias \citep{vanderwal2025} retrains \texttt{pythia-410m} from nine further random seeds, \texttt{pythia-410m-seed1} to \texttt{pythia-410m-seed9}, with the same code, data and schedule but a different initialisation and data order, and releases the same checkpoints; we call the original run seed 0. PolyPythias reports two of the nine, seeds 3 and 4, as outlier runs; we keep both and flag them, as fixed before any of their checkpoints was read.

Every seed and step reads the same seven texts: a passage of Camus's \emph{L'Étranger} in French, a method from the Python source of the HDBSCAN library, a passage of the \emph{Iliad} in English translation, the source of a LaTeX document, a paragraph from a paper on transformers, Sullivan Ballou's 1861 letter to his wife, and a Wikipedia paragraph on Charlotte Brontë, each cut at 512 tokens (prompt-set hash \texttt{976b62d677bc}). They span two languages, prose, verse, code and markup, but they are few and were chosen by hand, not sampled, and every count below is a count over these seven. Forward passes run in float32 with TF32 off, on one GPU. On seed 0 the per-state energies reproduce an earlier CPU run of this project to a relative 1.5 $\times 10^{-6}$, and the counts of \S\ref{sec:3.2} exactly.

\subsection{The energy and the drop count}\label{sec:3.2}

At each checkpoint and prompt we take the residual stream's 25 states as the model returns them: the token embeddings, the outputs of the first 23 blocks, and the last block's output after the final layer norm. Each token's vector is normalised to unit length, so a prompt's n tokens lie on the sphere, and $E_{1}$ of \S\ref{sec:2.1} ($\beta $ = 1) is computed at each state, every position included. A step from one state to the next is a \emph{drop} when $E_{1}$ falls by more than a fraction $10^{-3}$ of its value. \textbf{V}, the statistic of everything below, is the number of drops at one checkpoint: over the 24 transitions of each prompt, summed over the 7 prompts, so at most 24 $\times $ 7. Two of the transitions change frame as well as layer (the embeddings into the first block's output, and the last block's output into the final layer norm); they are counted like the rest, they hold none of the drops from step 256 to 1000 on any seed, and V counted without them dates every seed's break at the same step (appendix~\ref{sec:C}). V is not one prompt's: at each seed's break no prompt holds more than 0.24 of it (an even split is 0.14). And a drop is a dip, not a net fall: on every prompt, seed and step, $E_{1}$ is higher after the last state than at the embeddings (appendix~\ref{sec:C}, with figure~\ref{fig:fig5_count}'s map of where the drops sit). In the identity-weight picture V is 0. A drop is a departure from that picture's prediction, not the violation of a theorem (\S\ref{sec:2.1}). The tolerance keeps float noise out of the count; on seed 0 the break's date below is 512 at each of the tolerances $10^{-4}, 10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$, and 512 with any one prompt left out.

$\beta $ = 1 is the one setting seed 0 had been read at before the seeds were registered (\S\ref{sec:3.4}); no other $\beta $ is read in this frame. A second energy, $E_{3.5}$ in each block's input frame (after its first layer norm, positions 1 to n $-$ 1, blocks 0 to 23), is read beside it in \S\ref{sec:4} and never counted; $\beta $ = 3.5 is a value we fitted earlier to this model's attention scores, and the frame is the one attention reads.

\subsection{Dating the break}\label{sec:3.3}

Let m be the median of V over steps 1000, 2000 and 4000. The break's step is the first grid step at which V $\geq $ m / 2, a \emph{half-rise}. The plateau is early on purpose: V keeps moving late in training (on seed 0, 57 on the plateau and 70 at the last step), and a reference taken there would let a late rise move an early date. Read after the dates with the plateau at 8000--32 000 instead (median 59--74 drops by seed), every seed's half-rise stays in (128, 512] and only seed 9 moves, from 256 to 512 (appendix~\ref{sec:C}). Two other rules were set aside on seed 0 before the seeds were read: the first step with V > 0 moves with a single stray drop, and V's change-weighted centroid in log step lands at about 827 on seed 0, pulled late by the rise between 16 000 and 32 000.

\subsection{What was registered, and when}\label{sec:3.4}

Before any seed's checkpoints between its first and last were downloaded, we committed a prediction, \texttt{P-A5}, to this project's registry of predictions, kept in its git history: on each of the nine seeds the break falls at 256 or 512, a window (128, 512] chosen after seed 0 was read, with seed 0's step at its right edge. K counts the seeds inside. The registered falsifier was K $\leq $ 3, so under the null below the prediction escapes its falsifier (K $\geq $ 3 + 1) with probability 0.048. The null puts each seed's step uniformly and independently on the 12 non-zero grid steps up to 4000, so under it K \textasciitilde{} Bin(9, 1/6); p = P(Bin(9, 1/6) $\geq $ K), exact, and the e-value is E = $\kappa $ p\textasciicircum{}($\kappa -$1) with $\kappa $ = 0.5, a p-to-e calibrator fixed before the run \citep{vovk2019}. Nobody holds that null. It says the step is the run's, scattered over the early grid; it is the alternative to ``the step is the recipe's'', and beating it says only that. PolyPythias already reports training dynamics highly consistent across seeds, and every seed shares the recipe, so 9 of 9 was the expected outcome; what the registration adds is that the window was fixed before any seed's intermediate checkpoint was seen.

The registration commit (\texttt{c2eb9a7}) is dated 2026-10-20, 18:36 UTC; the first seed record was written at 18:39 UTC the same day, 3 minutes later. Both times are written by us: a commit's date is set by its author, and the record's time by our code. The earliest time outside our control is GitHub's activity log, which records the creation of the branch carrying the registration commit at 18:38 UTC, before the first seed record's 18:39; the log keeps such events for about 90 days, so a copy is kept with the code. Every seed's first and last checkpoints were already on disk, and earlier work of ours had read the first.

\textbf{Seed 0.} V is 0 at every step up to 128: on no prompt does $E_{1}$ fall by more than the tolerance from one of its 25 states to the next. It is 19 at 256 and 56 at 512, against a plateau median of 57, so the half-rise dates the break at \textbf{512} (figure~\ref{fig:fig1_break}), and V stays high to the end of training (70 at the last step).

\textbf{The nine seeds.} The break falls inside the window on all nine: \textbf{K = 9 of 9}, p = 9.9 $\times 10^{-8}$, E = 1587 against the uniform-location null. Seven seeds date it at 512, as seed 0 does; seeds 3 and 9 date it at 256. Both outlier runs, seeds 3 and 4, are inside. No seed is near the window's edges: at 128 every seed's V is at most 2, against half-plateau lines of at least 22; at 512 every V is at least 41, against lines of at most 29. A count that moved by one, the float-noise margin seed 0's gate allows, could move a seed between 256 and 512, never out of the window.

\textbf{What K = 9 says, and what it does not.} The break's step belongs to 410m's recipe, not to one run: the date \S\ref{sec:5} places on the developmental order is the recipe's. It does not separate a training event from the learning-rate schedule. Every seed shares the warmup, which runs to step 1430, past the whole window, so a break set by the schedule's ramp would reproduce exactly as this one does (\S\ref{sec:7}). And the null it beats is one nobody holds, so a large E here is cheap: the check makes the date reproducible, and is evidence neither about mechanism nor that trained weights resist the clustering the particle picture describes.

\textbf{Beside the count} (fixed before the run, never scored):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Step 0 is not monotone on every seed.} Seeds 1, 3, 5, 8 and 9 show 1--6 drops at step 0 (seed 0 none), up to 8 at step 4 (seed 3); V is 0 at steps 8 to 64 on every seed. Against plateaus of 44--58 these are small, but ``monotone at initialisation'' holds for seed 0 and four of the nine seeds, not for all ten. That the early drops are gone by step 8, the step the token cloud's rank collapse is dated (\S\ref{sec:5}), is seen, not read further.
  \item \emph{The second energy dates the break inside the window too.} $E_{3.5}$ in each block's input frame dates it at 512 on 9 of the 9 seeds, and at 512 on seed 0, so it differs from $E_{1}$'s date on seeds 3 and 9, which $E_{1}$ dates at 256.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The token-centred cloud}\label{sec:4.1}

$E_{1}$'s first-order term is $\tfrac12\|m\|^{2}$, the squared norm of the tokens' mean (\S\ref{sec:2.1}), so a direction all tokens share, growing or shrinking from layer to layer, moves $E_{1}$ with no change in how the tokens sit relative to one another (\S\ref{sec:8}). To remove it, at each state we take the unit token cloud, subtract the prompt's mean unit token and normalise each row again, and count the drops in $E_{1}$ of this centred cloud, $V_c$, with the same half-rise. (Normalising after centring leaves a small mean, so the centred cloud is not exactly mean-free.) The rule was fixed before any centred number was read. Centring changes $E_{1}$'s scale, so $V_c$ is counted at four tolerances: $10^{-4}$ and 3 $\times 10^{-4}$, fixed, and 7.6 $\times 10^{-5}$ and 7.6 $\times 10^{-4}$, derived from the uncentred count's behaviour, the first so that the same architecture at random initialisation drops as often centred as uncentred, the second so that a planted spread of the tokens is seen alike by both counts (appendix~\ref{sec:B}). A centred date counts only if it holds at all four. No single tolerance reads a planted change alike through both counts, so we compare their dates, never their sizes.

\textbf{Seed 0.} $V_c$ is at most 3 at every step through 128 across the four tolerances. At 512, where V is 0.98 of its plateau, $V_c$ is 0.19--0.24 of its own (5--11 drops); its half-rise is at \textbf{1000} at each tolerance. \textbf{The nine seeds.} The centred half-rise falls after the seed's uncentred break on eight of nine seeds, and at 1000 on eight; on seed 6 one of the four tolerances dates it at the seed's own break. Read after the dates: at the uncentred break $V_c$ is 0.07--0.50 of its own plateau on the seeds that break at 512 and 0.00--0.04 on the two that break at 256, below half on every seed but the one tie.

So, by the half-rise, the break at its step is not seen once the mean token is removed: the centred cloud has not yet broken at 256 or 512, and it breaks at 1000, the step where \S\ref{sec:5} dates the previous-token and induction heads. This is a statement about dates. It does not say the break is anisotropy. The two counts are one statistic on the same states, dated by one rule fixed before the run, which reads a later grid step as later; \S\ref{sec:5}'s convention, under which a one-step gap between rows of different kinds counts as aligned, is not applied to them.

\emph{The mean's term by layer} (read after the dates; description). Averaged over the prompts, $\|m\|^{2}$ never falls from one state to the next at step 128 on any of the ten seeds; at 512 it falls at 6--10 of its 24 transitions on every seed, and at 256 the two seeds whose break is at 256 have the most falls. At steps 128 to 1000 the two frame-changing transitions hold none of the falls (later in training they do). So $E_{1}$'s first-order term stops rising with depth at the break, on every seed. That is the same break seen through one term of $E_{1}$, not separate evidence.

\emph{How much of V the mean's term carries} (read after the dates; description). Since $E_{1} = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\|m\|^{2}$ + R exactly, with R $\geq $ 0 the sum of the higher terms (\S\ref{sec:2.1}; each is a squared norm), every change splits as $\Delta E_{1} = \tfrac12\Delta \|m\|^{2} + \Delta $R. The two terms move together almost always, and not only at the break: at every checkpoint, on the ten seeds and seven prompts, $E_{1}$ and $\|m\|^{2}$ change in the same direction at 0.96--1.00 of the transitions, and whenever $E_{1}$ falls by any amount $\|m\|^{2}$ falls at 0.96--1.00 of them. So V, at $\beta $ = 1, is close to a count of the layers at which the tokens' mean norm falls, at every step of training; the drops at the break are no exception ($\|m\|^{2}$ falls at each of the 1224 drops from step 128 to 1000, and its term is more than half the drop at all but one, the median share 0.81--1.02 by step; a share above 1 means R rose while the mean's term fell). The mean's term is not always most of $E_{1}$'s change: on the rises at steps 16 and 32 it is more than half at only 0.30 and 0.43 of them (from step 128 on, at most rises). The split is exact but it does not separate the mean from the rest: when the tokens share a direction every $\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle $ is large, so R's higher terms carry the mean too, and R is not a mean-free energy (the centred cloud nearly is, up to the small mean that re-normalising leaves). (Comparing these against R's own count of drops would need a tolerance on R's scale, which shrinks along training, and none is calibrated; it is not read.)

\section{Is the break the OV maps'?}\label{sec:6}

The monotonicity results of \S\ref{sec:2.1} need each head's value map tied to its symmetric scores, and the OV maps' attractive share falls at the break's step (\S\ref{sec:5}). So the first suspect is the OV maps turning away from symmetric, attracting maps. On seed 0 we edited every head's OV map at once in the forward pass and counted V again, at step 0, 256 and the five steps 512, 1000, 2000, 4000 and 143 000; only the last five are labelled. Two edits ask whether the break is the OV maps' asymmetry: (a) each map replaced by its symmetric part; (b) each map's rotation removed, keeping its gain: restricted to the head's write space, the map's real Schur form W = U S $U^{\top}$ (U orthogonal, S block upper-triangular) has each 2 $\times $ 2 diagonal block of S replaced by its symmetric part, so the rotation is removed plane by plane in an orthonormal basis even when the map is not normal. Each edit is read against 40 random edits matched to it twice: each control's change has the edit's norm, and each control's map has the edited map's norm, head by head. A cell is \emph{lower} or \emph{higher} only beyond all 40, and only beyond what the edit's change of gain alone does (the map rescaled to the edited norm, read beside and not drawn). The MLP blocks, which add to the stream in parallel with attention, are never edited. In-context copying is read beside it, as the repeat gain G of a probe of 16 sequences of 96 random tokens repeated once (the mean loss on the first copy's positions minus that on the second's), at the four steps from 1000, where copying exists.

\textbf{Neither edit undoes the break} (figure~\ref{fig:fig3_ov}). Under (a) V is lower than its controls at none of the five labelled steps and higher at 512 and 143 000; under (b) it is as its controls at every step. The controls show what (a) is: a large edit, random edits of its size alone moving V far from the unedited count and breaking most copying. Beside the labelled steps, (a) gives V = 11 at step 0, inside its controls' 0--17 (so a random edit of its size makes the initialised model non-monotone too), and 30 at 256, above all 40 (9--17). (b) is a small edit: removing the rotation changes a head's map by a median 0.08--0.10 of its norm. Copying survives it: G is 8.19--12.41 at the four steps, as its controls and about the unedited model's, where zeroing the OV maps of ten induction heads takes it to 1.06--2.57 (appendix~\ref{sec:B}).

\textbf{The one OV-only edit with a known destination does not reach it either.} Giving every head its own step-128 OV map, where V was 0, leaves V at 29--61 across the five steps. The edit is not inert: V is lower than every control at 512 (29 against 50--66) and at 1000 (31 against 38--58), and higher at 2000 (56 against 33--51), one grid step after the OV maps' attractive share reaches its lowest (\S\ref{sec:5}). The whole model moved to its step-128 weights, by contrast, gives V = 0 at every step from 512 to 4000. That is close to a tautology, since the step-128 model has V = 0 (\S\ref{sec:4}); it is a check that the count can register a reversal when one is made, not evidence about the OV maps. An OV-only edit does not take V there.

So the break is neither shown to be the OV maps' nor shown not to be. One OV-only edit with a known destination was tried, and it moves V in both directions without approaching monotone; the two structural edits show no reversal beyond random edits of their size. No other component (the MLPs, the layer norms, the embeddings, QK) was moved to its step-128 weights, so none of this is causal evidence about which components push the tokens apart.

\section{What would separate the schedule from training}\label{sec:7}

Every dated row of \S\ref{sec:5} from step 128 to 1000 falls inside the warmup, and the ten runs of \S\ref{sec:4} share it, so a break set by the ramp would reproduce exactly as this one does. Three kinds of run could separate the two: a model trained on the same schedule at a different peak rate; a suite with a different warmup and checkpoints inside it; and continued training from a checkpoint before the break, with and without the ramp. We ran the first two and read both dates, $E_{1}$'s break and the centred cloud's, by rules fixed before their outputs.

\textbf{The predictions.} Each schedule says where a date at 410m's step should fall on another run: under \emph{step}, at the same count of updates; under the \emph{cumulative} learning rate (the dose the weights have received), where that run's summed rate reaches 410m's at the date; under the \emph{instantaneous} rate, where its rate does. Each is mapped from 410m's dates on the ten seeds (256--512 for the break, 512--1000 for the centred date) onto the other run's grid as a band. A date g on the grid stands for a crossing somewhere in (g$'$, g], g$'$ the grid step before it; inside a linear warmup each schedule carries a step t on 410m to f$\cdot $t on the other run (f = 1 for step, $\surd $(p/p$'$) for the cumulative rate and p/p$'$ for the instantaneous one, p and p$'$ the two peak rates), and the band is every grid step s of the other run whose own interval (s$'$, s] meets (f$\cdot $g$'$, f$\cdot $g] for some date g in 410m's range. A run's dates keep the schedules whose bands contain them. The schedules are deterministic functions of the step, so a date consistent with one is not evidence that it causes anything.

\textbf{Pythia 70m and 160m.} They warm up over the same 1430 steps at peak rates of $10^{-3}$ and 6 $\times 10^{-4}$, against 3 $\times 10^{-4}$ for 410m, so the cumulative and instantaneous rates reach 410m's earlier in steps. On both, $E_{1}$'s break is at 256 and the centred date at 1000, the same at every tolerance (figure~\ref{fig:fig4_schedules}). Under the instantaneous rate the centred date is predicted at 256--512 on 160m and 128--512 on 70m, so the rate itself is excluded; on 160m step predicts 512--1000 and the cumulative rate 256--1000, both containing 1000. $E_{1}$'s break at 256 excludes none of the three: all their bands contain it. The joint reading is \textbf{consistent with step and cumulative}, and it is the same without 70m's $E_{1}$ date, which rests on a plateau of 6 drops, so a few drops move it. Step against the cumulative dose is not told apart on Pythia's grid; size changes with the rate, and there is one seed per size (PolyPythias also releases seeds of 70m and 160m, not read here).

\textbf{Mistral GPT-2 small.} Stanford CRFM's Mistral trained five seeds of a GPT-2 small (its states taken as for Pythia: the embeddings, each block's output, the last after the final layer norm) with checkpoints every 10 steps to 100, every 50 to 2000 and more sparsely after \citep{crfm}. Its learning rate rises logarithmically, reaching half its peak of 6 $\times 10^{-4}$ by step 100 and the peak at about step 4000, as logged in each seed's released trainer state, so the cumulative dose predicts far earlier dates than step does, and here the schedules separate: for $E_{1}$'s break the cumulative band is 40--150, step's 150--550. $E_{1}$'s break falls at 250 or 350 on all five seeds, in step's band only, and the frozen joint reading is \textbf{``consistent with step''}. The rule fixed before the run said this label would narrow the claim that nothing separates the schedule from training. We decided after reading the run, and before writing this section, not to let it, for three reasons:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{$E_{1}$ rises in two stages} on every seed (figure~\ref{fig:fig4_schedules}): to 14--17 drops by step 100, inside the cumulative band, then to 31--39 by 350, inside step's. The frozen plateau (the median over 4000--16 000: 410m's window carried by tokens seen, since a GPT-2 small step sees a quarter of a Pythia step's tokens) puts its half between the stages; against 410m's own window, 1000--4000, the half-rise reads 80 or 200, and the joint would read ``neither''. The label holds for the larger stage and depends on the window.
  \item \emph{The centred count gives no date.} Each seed's four tolerances disagree, from 100--2000: the two fixed tolerances jump at 100--150, in the cumulative band, the two derived ones rise slowly and date 350--2000. By the rule a disagreement widens a seed's date to its span, and step survives the centred count on four seeds only through the tolerance matched to the re-initialised models, which is set by their single largest drop; at the next value the joint reads ``neither''.
  \item \emph{Neither schedule maps the whole curve.} Under step, 410m has nothing at GPT-2's first stage (V is 0 through 128); under the cumulative dose, GPT-2's first stage maps onto most of 410m's rise, where GPT-2 is still far below its plateau.
\end{itemize}

So on another suite with a different warmup, the break's larger rise falls where the count of updates predicts, after a first rise where the dose does, and the centred cloud's single date of \S\ref{sec:4.1} does not transfer. Nothing here separates a training event from the learning-rate schedule on \texttt{pythia-410m}. The run that could is the third kind: continued training from a checkpoint before the break with the ramp replaced by a flat rate, on a model whose circuit is known.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:8}

\textbf{Geometry along the training axis.} Several works read a global geometric statistic of hidden states along Pythia's checkpoints. \citet{li2025} find three phases in the spectrum of the last layer's representations (RankMe, $\alpha $ReQ), the first coinciding with the learning-rate ramp. \citet{billa2026} finds task representations' RankMe collapsing from step 0 to steps 32--256 on Pythia 160m and 410m and partly recovering by about step 1000. \citet{cheng2024} follow intrinsic dimension by layer from step 512, and \citet{razzhigaev2023} report intrinsic dimension rising and then falling over training. A non-monotone geometric arc along training is therefore not new, and neither is the statistic in kind: at $\beta $ = 1, $E_{1}$'s first-order term is $\tfrac12\|m\|^{2}$, half the anisotropy measure $\|m\|^{2}$ (\S\ref{sec:2.1}). What is new here is reading the particle picture's prediction, $E_\beta $ monotone in depth, along a real training axis: the step at which it breaks, that step's reproduction across seeds, and its place on the developmental order.

\textbf{Anisotropy, outlier dimensions and sinks.} $E_\beta $ is a moment of the tokens' Gram matrix, so it moves with anisotropy, the tokens' shared direction. Contextual representations are anisotropic in every layer \citep{ethayarajh2019}; a few rogue dimensions dominate similarity measures between them \citep{timkey2021}; trained transformers' representations are dominated by one principal direction \citep{benartzy2026}, and decoders' anisotropy peaks in their middle layers \citep{razzhigaev2023}; \S\ref{sec:4.1}'s profile of $\|m\|^{2}$, rising with depth before the break and falling at several transitions after it, is compatible with such a peak forming at the break, though we did not locate the peak on the seeds. Large language models carry a few activations of very large magnitude that act as fixed biases \citep{sun2024}, and attention concentrates on initial tokens that act as sinks \citep{xiao2023}; either could carry the mean direction \S\ref{sec:4.1} removes, and neither is examined here beyond appendix~\ref{sec:A}'s note on the massive coordinates. Along Pythia's training, \citet{bernas2026} study anisotropy chiefly through the gradients, with activations read from step 10 000 on; we found no reviewed measurement of activation anisotropy inside the break's window. \S\ref{sec:4.1} reports the energy on token-centred clouds, the control this calls for. Pure attention loses rank with depth \citep{dong2021}, and rank collapse at initialisation hinders training \citep{noci2022}; our row E5 reads the token cloud's rank along training.

\textbf{Warmup.} The warmup is commonly explained as controlling the adaptive optimiser's early variance \citep{liu2019} or as moving the model to regions that tolerate larger learning rates \citep{kalra2024}. Both make the schedule a plausible cause of any event inside it, which is why \S\ref{sec:7} does not separate the two.

\textbf{Developmental interpretability.} The stagewise picture of \S\ref{sec:2.2} comes from the local learning coefficient \citep{hoogland2024}, refined per head \citep{wang2024}, and from susceptibilities \citep{wang2025}; induction heads \citep{olsson2022} and the function-vector heads many of them become \citep{yin2025} are its best-studied circuit, and how an induction head forms has been studied mechanistically \citep{singh2024}. Abrupt structure formation during training, with a sudden drop in the loss, is reported in masked language models \citep{chen2023}. PolyPythias \citep{vanderwal2025} reports training dynamics highly consistent across seeds, with outlier runs; \S\ref{sec:4}'s seeds include two such runs.

\textbf{Theory.} The particle picture of self-attention, its clustering and metastability, are developed in \citet{geshkovski2023,geshkovski2024}, \citet{bruno2024} and \citet{rigollet2025}; the monotonicity conditions of \S\ref{sec:2.1} are in \citet{geshkovski2023} and \citet{pendharkar2026}. \citet{isobe2026} train a noisy mean-field model's feed-forward part and find tokens leaving the clustered regime near the final layers, a predicted shape with no checkpoints. \citet{herty2026} couple the tokens' flow in depth with the parameters' flow in training, as theory.

\textbf{Earlier notes.} The author's informal notes compared trained GPT-2, ALBERT and BERT models with random weights of matched norm: under random weights the energy rose monotonically in depth, as the identity-weight picture predicts, and in every trained model, prompt and $\beta $ tested it did not. They read only endpoints; \S\ref{sec:4} dates when, along training, the monotonicity is lost.

\section{The shared subspaces}\label{sec:A}

Do the 384 heads come to query, key, read and write the same directions of the residual stream, and when? For each family of per-head subspaces (query, key, OV write, OV read, and the OV map's invariant subspaces with negative real, positive real and complex eigenvalues) we average the heads' orthogonal projectors and take the participation ratio PR of the mean. Under a null that rotates each head's subspace by an independent Haar rotation PR has an exact expected value $\operatorname{PR}_{0}$, and $\rho $ = PR / $\operatorname{PR}_{0}$ is 1 under it and falls as the heads concentrate on shared directions. Each step's PR is read against the null's range over its draws. The Haar null, a Gaussian draw of the weights matched in norm, and the step-0 weights give the same distribution of PR, and every step is read against it. $\rho $ is a description: a count of shared directions cannot be calibrated on a trained pool, and neither can $\rho $'s effect side, because a planted shared direction added to the pool lowers $\rho $ mostly by diluting the pool's own concentration, so a planted effect's size says nothing about a real one. The readings use the weights only, except the comparison with the residual stream's top-variance directions below, which uses the seven prompts. $\rho $ by family and step is not tabulated here.

Through step 128 every family reads the null (|$\rho $ $-$ 1| at most 9.4 $\times 10^{-4}$ in the OV families and 4.5 $\times 10^{-4}$ in QK). The query and key families leave it at 256, with the OV write (256) and OV complex (256) families; OV read, negative and positive follow at 512. All of this is inside the warmup, at or before the break. OV write is the most concentrated family at 512 and 1000 ($\rho $ 0.89 and 0.66); from 2000 the query family is ($\rho $ 0.50), and from 4000 the query and key families both are, ending at 0.51--0.53 against the OV families' 0.65--0.82 at the last step. The shared spaces are not the residual stream's top-variance directions at any step, and at the last step the OV write, negative and complex ones partly coincide with 410m's massive-activation coordinates, which they avoid through 4000.

\citet{shaikh2026} read an overlap of heads' projections in the weights of four larger models, with checkpoints on OLMo-2 only: keys grow most, queries the same way more gently, values stay near-orthogonal throughout. Our key family agrees in direction. Their value statistic is not our OV read family (a different subspace, pooled differently), so the two are not compared.

\section{The gates, and why each null is the one used}\label{sec:B}

Each reading above was gated before it was read: the full statistic was run on inputs whose answer is known, on a planted null and on a planted effect at the real design points, and the real data were read only once every arm passed. Where an arm failed, the failure and the change are stated where the reading is.

\textbf{The break's half-rise (\S\ref{sec:3.3}, \S\ref{sec:4}; the gate of \texttt{P-A5} on seed 0).} \emph{Known answer:} seed 0's energies reproduce an earlier CPU run to a relative 1.5 $\times 10^{-6}$ (the gate's tolerance $10^{-4}$), and V exactly. \emph{Planted breaks:} a synthetic series breaking at each grid step is read at that step for 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1000, 2000; one planted at 4000, the plateau's last step, reads 2000, since the plateau then contains the rise; a series is read inside the window exactly when its break was planted there. \emph{Planted null:} 20 000 draws of the nine seeds' steps from the uniform-location null, through the scorer, reproduce Bin(9, 1/6) for K (largest standardised gap 2.4); this checks the scorer, and the planted breaks check the half-rise rule. \emph{Planted effect:} seed 0's series moved one grid step later reads 1000, outside the window. \emph{Sensitivity:} the date is the same at every tolerance and with any one prompt left out (\S\ref{sec:3.2}). \emph{Why this null:} it is the alternative to ``the recipe sets the step'', scattering each run's step over the early grid. Nobody holds it, which is why beating it is cheap (\S\ref{sec:4}).

\textbf{The locator (\S\ref{sec:5}).} Each row's no-change null (bootstrap draws of the row with its change removed) reads ``no change'' in at least 0.972 of 1000 draws, and changes planted in each gap of the grid at the row's own noise are placed in the right gap. The first gate failed on four rows whose spread grows by orders of magnitude over training; the revised gate, with each row's floor on its own spread, passes on every row dated, and the simulator's row (E11) is refused. \emph{Why:} a date is only as good as the locator's power to tell a change from none in each gap.

\textbf{The OV edits (\S\ref{sec:6}).} Random edits are never labelled lower or higher against their own controls (G1: 0 labels). Zeroing ten induction heads' OV maps loses copying at four of four steps (G2: G 1.06--2.57). Moving the whole model to its step-128 weights lowers V below its controls at four steps at full dose, to V = 0 (G3), so the count can see a reversal when one is made. A displacement guard, meant to leave unread any cell whose edit moves the residual stream further than its controls, refused both planted effects, and was made description before any arm was read; it is not reported here. \emph{Why random edits matched in size and in each head's norm:} a structured edit has to be told from what any edit of its size does, and the gain change alone is read beside, because the scale of a head's map moves V by itself.

\textbf{The centred counts (\S\ref{sec:4.1}, \S\ref{sec:7}).} The steps no schedule reaches (0--128 on Pythia, 0 and 10 on GPT-2 small) are read first, and a centred count there of half its plateau or more would refuse the control; none did. The two derived tolerances are fixed per model before its records: one so that the re-initialised models' centred drop rate matches their uncentred one (the same architecture with fresh random weights; on GPT-2 small this tolerance is set by a single largest drop of theirs), one so that a planted spread is seen by the centred count as by the uncentred one; each tolerance is gated on the re-initialised models and on two planted effects (a common vector added to every token, which the centred count must not see, and a spread, which it must). One tolerance on the centred energy's own scale, meant to compare the two counts' sizes, failed its own gate, which is why only dates are compared. The centred rules were frozen after a preflight (\texttt{tools.design\_preflight}), which runs the statistic on step 0, a matched random initialisation, the trained population at a step off the scored grid, and a planted control at dose 0 and three doses; its refusals are fixed or argued in the design.

So a drop is a dip inside a net rise, not a fall of the whole profile.

\textbf{A later plateau.} With the plateau taken at 8000, 16 000 and 32 000 the dates are 256 and 512; only seed 9 moves, from 256 to 512.

\textbf{Which coordinates carry the mean} (read after every date; description; a read-out of the states, not an intervention).
