## 1. The question, and the lens

The theory this project tests proves clustering for **chosen** weights (identity-like, symmetric
QK, tied layers). Random-weight theory covers **step 0**. Pythia's trained weights are neither,
and nothing in the repo describes **the path** between them (`FUTURE_IDEAS.md` §0, "the
selection map").

The lens (geometric deep learning's, turned onto the parameters): a parameter space Θ, a group
G of transforms that leave the computed function unchanged, and the function living on Θ / G.
Three consequences frame every question below:

| # | Consequence | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | "The trained weights" are a G-orbit, not a point; only G-invariant quantities compare runs, seeds and the three reference points | random / identity / trained become three places in the quotient, each comparable by invariants |
| L2 | Each continuous symmetry gives a **conservation law** under gradient flow (Noether-type) | conserved quantities pin the flow to a leaf fixed by the init: an exact form of "the parameters shrink into a subset" |
| L3 | The **optimiser** need not respect G: weight decay, finite steps, Adam's coordinate-wise scaling break the laws, each in its own way | the drift of the conserved quantities is a fingerprint of how the optimiser, not the data, shapes the trained set |

part of Ψ_QK; and the gain law F3 becomes γ_i² + β_i² − ‖W[:, i]‖² with the LayerNorm bias β.

**F5 against the repo's data** (`data/analysis/qk_symmetry_sweep.json`, block J, 410m,

an open measurement (§5 E1).

## 6. Telling tractable from intractable before investing

1. **Does a small set of variables close on itself?** (M with balanced init does; one head inside a trained network does not.) If predicting one parameter needs all of them, stop.
2. **Is there a limit where it closes** (many heads, small η, small init), and is Pythia near it?
3. **Does a solvable toy reproduce the phenomenon?** If it vanishes in every solvable toy, the toy's theory will not transfer.
4. **Can it be checked numerically in an afternoon** before it is proved? Q1–Q6 can.

**The distinction the readings turn on** (reworded, finding 4): Adam leaves alone a null
direction that is one coordinate, and the difference of two coordinates that share gradient,
init and optimiser state (the bias pair). It does not keep a zero-sum constraint over
coordinates whose gradients differ (a mean over the vocabulary or over the stream's
coordinates): the per-coordinate normalised update of a zero-sum gradient is not zero-sum
(D7). SGD conserves these exactly.

### 8.3 Adam's motion law and its two clocks (Q3)

**D3.** For one coordinate with gradient μ + σξ_t and ρ = μ/√(μ² + σ²), over a slowly varying
rate: displacement = ρ τ₁ ± (1 − ρ²)√(1 − ρ²/2) √τ₂, with **τ₁ = Σ η_t, τ₂ = Σ η_t²**. So
\|Δθ\| ≤ τ₁; a consistent sign travels τ₁; a zero-mean gradient walks √τ₂. In the small-rate
limit the drift part of Adam's path is a function of τ₁ alone (the "summed rate" clock), the
diffusive part of τ₂, and only the averaging windows (10 and 20 steps) count in steps. On a
linear ramp of length W: τ₁ = η̂ t²/2W, τ₂ = η̂² t³/3W².

### 9.2 AdamW in integral form (D9)

With u_t = m̂_t / (√v̂_t + ε) (Adam's normalised update, \|u_t\| ≲ 1) and decoupled decay λ:

**θ_T = A θ_0 − Σ_t w_t u_t, A = Π_t (1 − η_t λ) ≈ e^{−λτ₁}, w_t = η_t Π_{s>t} (1 − η_s λ).**

It is an identity of the update rule. What it gives:

| # | Consequence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| a | The trained weight is the init's remnant plus a decay-weighted sum of normalised updates; the window is 1/λ = 10 units of τ₁ | τ₁(143000) is 74 (70m), 22 (410m): 70m's weights hold about the last seventh of the run's summed rate |
| b | Two checkpoints a < b give ū = −(θ_b − A_ab θ_a) / Σw **exactly**; a weight that stands still has ū = −λθ | every row of 9.3's interval table; 8.4's readings are this identity for a bias (λ = 0) and for a linear functional |
| c | A linear functional c with c·u_t = 0 at every step decays as A. Under sign steps on f(uv) that is \|u\| − \|v\| (rate λ; gradient flow's u² − v² goes at 2λ, F2) | Q4, scalar factors |
| d | For \|g\| ≪ ε the step is η g / ε: Adam is SGD with rate η/ε, linear, and keeps zero-sum constraints (it does not above ε, D7) | E0's early window |

### 9.3 The first update from the init (Q8; D10)

for bit, both models) and checkpoint 2 holds the first update that moves. Adam's first updates
are sign steps (known; e.g. 2412.02153), so Δθ = −η u, u ≈ sign(g). At the init the logits are
near uniform, so the gradient at the unembedding's input is Σ_t W_Uᵀ(p_t − e_{y_t}) ≈
−N W_Uᵀ(u − 1/V), with u the batch's token frequencies. LayerNorm's Jacobian removes the part
along **1** and along the token's own stream (D10b), which is where the model's own prediction

step is rank one: sign(mean back-signal) ⊗ sign(mean input).** The toy in D10d has this
structure in its exact gradient.

### 9.4 F6 is complete on a toy (Q2; D11)

**So:** in exact arithmetic b_K is invisible (G0). In the float32 graph every reading here uses, it
is **the main source of rounding** in the top layers. It raises float32's floor there 25–60 × in
the residual stream and 50–65 × in the attention, to ≈ 10⁻⁴ relative. Its cost to the loss is
10⁻⁶ nats, far below every effect a reading here reports (the positive control's 0.056–1.3
nats). A reading can move only if its output is
sensitive at 10⁻⁴ relative in the top layers: a threshold count with a point near its tolerance,
or a tie order. Which ones (if any) actually move is **not tested**. **Practical consequence:**
zeroing KBn before a float32 reading is exact in real arithmetic and removes most of the floor.
Doing so would change recorded outputs at the last digits, so it is not adopted here.

**Not established.** That any recorded reading moves (no reading was re-run). Other steps (at
256, b_K is 0.37 τ₁ on 70m: smaller, not measured). Rows longer than 512 or the prompt battery.
Why 70m's fp16 error is mostly not b_K. That the stand-in batch is representative.

**The answer rule had one reachable outcome (`LESSONS.md` 6; `/challenge-pr` on #357, finding
1).** With δ_I and δ_Z the intact and zeroed arms' logit errors against exact, the KLs are
quadratic to second order. So r = d / e_I ≈ 1 + e_Z / e_I − ρ, with ρ = 2 Cov_p(δ_I, δ_Z) / Var_p(δ_I) (D14,
`tools/math_checks/ts_bk_zero.py`; second order only).
The records fit this: on 410m R32, e_Z / e_I = 0.0043 and r = 1.0033 give ρ ≈ 0.001. Take the
null where b_K plays no part in the rounding. Zeroing still changes the bits, so the errors are
the same size and uncorrelated, and r ≈ 2: "comparable", which the answer rule maps to "matters".
If b_K *is* the floor, r ≈ 1: "comparable" again. "Cosmetic" (r ≤ 1 with e_Z ≈ e_I) needs the
rounding to be nearly bit-identical after the edit, and "matters" (r ≥ 10) needs an effect
larger than the floor. Neither is what the question posed. So the 162 (b) answer was fixed
before the run. G0 tested the zeroing, not the label rule: the calibration gate (a planted null
through the rule) was skipped, and the 1 / 10 / 0.5 / 2 thresholds were set without placing them.
The informative statistic is e_Z / e_I, fixed only as description. Its 0.5 bar is also unplaced,
but the readings (0.004–0.34 on R32) are far from it.

**Class per output (V at each τ; each pick), from three values: R32,I, R32,Z, R64,I.**

| Class | When | Reads |

All three are reachable, and which one occurs depends on μ_τ against δ_I and δ_Z, which nothing

μ_τ > δ_I and "floor-fragile" otherwise. It never reads "b_K reaches it", except by the chance that
other bits round back to exact. The flipped arm (s = −1) is the beside check on that chance. This
answers `/challenge-pr` on #357, finding 1: that rule could return one label.

**Gates, in order.**
- **K (the record).** R32,I reproduces the recorded `data/trackA/a5/2026-10-20-u1/seed0/records/step143000_*.json`:
